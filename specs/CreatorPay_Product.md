# CreatorPay — Product Definition and Business Strategy

**Document status:** Working product definition for team alignment  
**Product name:** CreatorPay  
**Primary launch market:** Nigeria  
**Longer-term market:** Other English-speaking African markets and suitable international markets after validation  
**Product category:** B2B creator-campaign management and performance-rewards platform  
**Primary paying customer:** Businesses running recurring creator-marketing campaigns  
**Secondary paying customer:** Agencies managing creator campaigns for multiple businesses  
**Essential product users:** Creators participating in campaigns

> **Purpose of this document:** Establish one clear definition of CreatorPay: what it is, which problem it solves, who it serves, what it does, how its rules work, how it makes money, how it reaches customers, and what the team must validate before treating assumptions as facts.

---

# 1. Executive definition

## 1.1 What CreatorPay is

**CreatorPay is a creator campaign management and performance rewards platform that helps businesses manage creator partnerships, measure the outcomes they can attribute to participating creators, and calculate and coordinate the rewards those creators earn.**

Businesses use CreatorPay to create campaigns, define objectives and compensation terms, invite creators, coordinate deliverables, track available campaign activity, review submissions, verify eligible outcomes, calculate rewards, and manage settlement statuses.

Creators use CreatorPay to receive or apply for campaigns, read the terms they are being asked to accept, submit content, access their own campaign tracking links, review attributable results, understand reward calculations, and see payment statuses.

Both sides work from one campaign record. The record connects agreed terms to deliverables, available evidence, reward calculations, and settlement history.

CreatorPay is therefore **a platform supported by tracking, verification, reward-calculation, and integration infrastructure**. It is not best introduced to customers as infrastructure alone, because the primary customer experience is a usable campaign-management product. The infrastructure is what makes the connected product possible and extensible.

## 1.2 Brand positioning

**Tagline:** Make creator marketing accountable.

**Hook:** Know what your creators deliver, not just what they post.

**One-sentence value proposition:** CreatorPay gives businesses a shared system to manage creator campaigns, substantiate attributable outcomes, and apply agreed compensation rules transparently.

**Core promise:** From campaign agreement to measurable outcomes to transparent rewards.

## 1.3 The product in one sentence

The business defines the campaign and the terms; the creator delivers the agreed work; CreatorPay records eligible events and evidence, applies the documented rules, and keeps the resulting reward and settlement record visible to the right people.

## 1.4 The core workflow

**Define → Manage → Track → Verify → Calculate → Settle → Learn.**

Every proposed feature should strengthen one or more steps in this workflow. If a feature does not help a business operate a campaign, a creator participate in one, or the platform reliably account for evidence and rewards, it needs a separate justification.

# 2. The problem we are solving

## 2.1 The central problem

Businesses and creators often lack a dependable shared process connecting campaign agreements, deliverables, attributable outcomes, and compensation.

Creator marketing has several different activities that need to agree with each other: a business decides what it wants, a creator agrees to deliver certain work, content is published, customers react, results are recorded, and compensation is determined. In many workflows, those stages are handled in different tools and by different people. The result is a fragmented record and an unreliable basis for evaluating performance or resolving disagreements.

CreatorPay's job is to create a connected, auditable workflow without pretending that every event or sale can be tracked perfectly.

## 2.2 Problem one: businesses cannot consistently connect activity to outcomes

Creator content can generate reach, comments, shares, clicks, enquiries, registrations, purchases, or longer-term awareness. These actions are not interchangeable. A campaign with an awareness objective should not be judged exactly like a campaign meant to produce registrations or sales.

Businesses may collect screenshots, platform metrics, links, promo-code results, website analytics, and sales exports, but these records do not always identify what each participating creator contributed. The business may see that a campaign was active without being able to compare creators using the same outcome definition.

**Customer frustration:** “We spent money on creators, but we still cannot confidently explain what each creator delivered against the objective we agreed on.”

**Commercial impact:** Weak budget allocation, inconsistent reporting, time spent reconciling evidence, and difficulty learning which approaches to repeat.

## 2.3 Problem two: campaign operations are fragmented

Campaign briefs, deliverables, deadlines, content approvals, performance updates, and payment records commonly exist across messaging apps, spreadsheets, emails, bank-transfer references, and reporting dashboards. The more creators and campaigns a business manages, the more work is required to keep those records aligned.

**Customer frustration:** “I have to chase people and combine multiple sources just to understand the status of a campaign.”

**Commercial impact:** Administrative overhead, missed deadlines, inconsistent information, weak oversight, and delayed campaign reporting.

## 2.4 Problem three: performance and compensation lack a shared record

Businesses need evidence that the agreed work was delivered and that reported outcomes meet the campaign's requirements. Creators need clear terms and visibility into the work accepted, results attributed to them, reward calculations, and payment status.

If the rules, evidence, and decisions are not recorded together, a business may struggle to defend its assessment and a creator may struggle to establish their claim. Even when both sides act in good faith, incomplete records can create avoidable disputes.

**Customer frustration:** “We do not have one agreed record showing what was promised, what happened, and how the final amount was calculated.”

**Commercial impact:** Lower trust, time spent resolving disputes, uncertainty about outstanding obligations, and weaker creator relationships.

## 2.5 Problem four: past campaign learning is lost

If businesses define objectives differently, collect evidence inconsistently, and maintain reports in different formats, campaign comparisons become unreliable. A business may not know whether a creator performed well because of the audience, creative format, destination page, offer, or the way results were measured.

**Customer frustration:** “We keep running campaigns, but every report requires manual work and it is still difficult to decide who should receive more budget.”

**Commercial impact:** Repeated guesswork and a limited ability to improve the next campaign.

## 2.6 Why this matters in Nigeria

Creator marketing in Nigeria often spans multiple social channels and the communication or purchasing channels around them. Discovery may happen on TikTok or Instagram, a question may move to WhatsApp, and the eventual registration or purchase may happen on a website, through a code, or outside a system that CreatorPay can observe directly. This creates attribution limits that must be handled honestly.

The Nigerian market also has businesses and creators with different levels of technical sophistication. Some brands can integrate event tracking; others need simple tracked links, supported imports, promotional codes, and review workflows. A useful product must meet customers where their capabilities are today while making better measurement possible where the necessary evidence exists.

DottsMediaHouse's 2026 Nigeria Influencer Marketing Report describes the market as becoming more structured and data-driven, and highlights the importance of measurement, fraud mitigation, and accountability. Its public market commentary estimated Nigerian influencer-marketing spend at ₦161.4 billion across the research period it discusses. That is an industry estimate, not CreatorPay's addressable software revenue. See [DottsMediaHouse's 2026 trend summary](https://dottsmediahouse.com/top-influencer-marketing-trends-to-know-in-2026/) and [NIMR 2026](https://dottsmediahouse.com/nimr/).

The IAB's 2025 Creator Economy Ad Spend & Strategy Report states that U.S. creator advertising investment is growing and calls out measurement, attribution, standardisation, and operational tools as areas needing improvement. Its forecast of $37 billion is for **U.S. creator ad spend**, not the global market. It supports the relevance of the problem but should not be conflated with Nigerian market figures. Source: [IAB 2025 report](https://www.iab.com/insights/2025-creator-economy-ad-spend-strategy-report/).

## 2.7 What the problem is not

CreatorPay is not primarily solving a lack of creator supply, a lack of social platforms, or a lack of payment methods. Those issues may affect particular customers, but they are not the centre of the proposition.

The core problem is **campaign accountability**: the absence of a dependable process linking the agreed work to evidence, outcomes, reward calculations, and settlement.

# 3. Product identity and boundaries

## 3.1 Product category

CreatorPay is a **B2B creator campaign management and performance rewards platform**.

It includes:

1. A business campaign-management workspace.
2. A creator participation portal.
3. A tracking and attribution layer.
4. A submission and outcome-verification workflow.
5. A reward calculation and settlement-status ledger.
6. Reporting and integration capabilities.

## 3.2 Is it a marketplace?

A marketplace is not the primary product identity. CreatorPay can support public campaign opportunities and creator applications, but the core product must still work when a business brings creators it already knows.

A business should not have to recruit its creators through CreatorPay before it can use the campaign-management and accountability workflow. This helps us sell into existing behaviour instead of first having to build a large two-sided network.

Discovery, creator recommendations, social-data enrichment, and broad searchable creator databases may be useful later, but they should not displace the core value proposition.

## 3.3 Is it infrastructure?

Infrastructure is part of the product but not the entire customer-facing product. The tracking, event ingestion, verification, rules, and reward engine support the dashboard and can be exposed through APIs to customers that need integrations.

The public positioning should stay understandable: CreatorPay is the platform businesses use to manage, measure, and account for creator campaigns.

## 3.4 What CreatorPay is not

- **Not a social network:** creators do not join to publish general content to followers.
- **Not an agency by default:** the main business is software, not personally sourcing, negotiating, and executing every campaign.
- **Not discovery-first:** businesses can bring their own creators; campaign applications can be supported where useful.
- **Not a universal social analytics product:** data availability depends on permitted platform access and integrations.
- **Not a guarantee of perfect attribution:** events are only attributable where tracking and evidence support that conclusion.
- **Not automatically an escrow or money-custody provider:** payment execution and custody require an appropriate provider and legal review.
- **Not performance-only compensation:** a campaign may pay a fixed fee for approved work, a performance reward, or a combination.
- **Not a Web3 product requirement:** the core value does not depend on blockchain or tokens.

# 4. Customers, users, and stakeholders

## 4.1 Primary paying customer: businesses

Businesses are the primary customer because they own the campaign objective and marketing budget, coordinate the creator relationship, and receive the reporting and operational benefits.

### Ideal customer profile

Prioritise businesses that:

- Run creator campaigns repeatedly rather than occasionally.
- Work with multiple creators or coordinate multiple deliverables.
- Have a measurable objective such as qualified leads, registrations, purchases, or completed actions.
- Can record the relevant action through a website, application, CRM, order system, promo code, export, or other supported evidence method.
- Need to explain campaign performance to a founder, leadership, client, or finance team.
- Currently reconcile campaign information manually or across disconnected products.
- Want to keep using their current creator relationships rather than replace them with a new marketplace.

Potential initial categories include e-commerce and consumer brands, apps and digital services, online education and subscription products, and event/ticketing businesses. They are not equally suitable: prioritise customers whose relevant outcomes can be recorded with acceptable evidence.

## 4.2 Secondary paying customer: agencies

Agencies manage creator campaigns for multiple client businesses and may face repeat coordination, reporting, approval, and reconciliation work. They can be customers in their own right and a distribution channel that introduces CreatorPay to multiple brands.

Agency features must support client separation and permission boundaries. An agency should see only the clients and campaigns it is authorised to manage.

## 4.3 Essential product user: creators

Creators use the platform to participate in campaigns and manage their side of the commercial relationship. They need clear campaign terms, briefs, submission statuses, their own performance records, reward breakdowns, and payment statuses.

Creators are essential to adoption but are not the primary subscription payer in the proposed business model. Basic campaign participation and access to one's own relevant records should not require a paid creator subscription.

## 4.4 Consumers

Consumers who click a creator's campaign link do not need a CreatorPay account. Their job is to get to the advertised business, not join another platform.

## 4.5 Internal users

Platform administrators and support staff need controlled tools for account support, suspicious activity review, operational monitoring, and dispute handling. Administrative access to campaign or payment data must be permissioned and logged.

# 5. Value proposition by customer

## 5.1 For businesses

- Create campaign rules before creators participate.
- Keep creator invitations, deliverables, content reviews, events, rewards, and settlement history connected.
- Measure eligible outcomes where reliable evidence is available.
- Compare creators using consistent campaign objectives and metrics.
- Reduce manual campaign reporting and reconciliation.
- Retain the ability to bring existing creator relationships.
- Use campaign history to make more informed budget decisions.

The product does not guarantee a positive return on every campaign. It provides a better process and more defensible evidence for assessing the results.

## 5.2 For creators

- Understand deliverables, deadlines, attribution rules, and compensation before accepting a campaign.
- Submit work and see review status without relying entirely on messages.
- Access campaign-specific links and their own attributable results.
- Understand how rewards were calculated.
- Distinguish earned, pending, approved, disputed, and paid amounts.
- Build a history of delivered work and substantiated outcomes.

A performance record must distinguish verified information from self-reported or incomplete information. It must not inflate claims about what a creator caused.

## 5.3 For agencies

- Manage multiple client campaigns through a consistent workflow.
- Coordinate briefs, submissions, review, and reporting in one place.
- Produce comparable reports across campaigns.
- Reduce time spent reconciling screenshots, exports, and chat messages.
- Preserve appropriate separation between clients.

# 6. Complete feature catalogue

The feature catalogue describes the intended product capability set. It is not a limited MVP list. Implementation may be phased, but no feature should contradict the rules in this document.

## 6.1 Business accounts and workspaces

- Business registration and workspace creation.
- Organisation profile and contact details.
- Account settings and notifications.
- Team-member invitations.
- Role-based permissions for workspace owner, administrator, campaign manager, content reviewer, finance user, and read-only analyst.
- Workspace membership and offboarding.
- Campaign ownership and access controls.
- Workspace activity history.
- Billing account and subscription status.
- Secure separation between unrelated businesses.

## 6.2 Agency and multi-client management

- Agency workspace and profile.
- Authorised connection to client business workspaces.
- Client-specific roles and permissions.
- Campaign views filtered by client.
- Cross-client operational dashboards where the agency is authorised.
- Client-specific reporting and exports.
- Clear client association for campaign budgets, rewards, and invoices.
- Audit logs for actions taken on a client's behalf.
- No cross-client data exposure by default.

## 6.3 Campaign creation and configuration

A campaign can include:

- Campaign name, summary, and long description.
- Business identity, branding, banner, and campaign assets.
- Product or service being promoted.
- Campaign objective or combination of objectives.
- Target market and audience description.
- Supported content platforms and formats.
- Required deliverables and delivery deadlines.
- Creator eligibility conditions.
- Participation method: invite-only, application-based, or a combination.
- Campaign start and end dates.
- Application or participation deadlines where needed.
- Landing-page or final destination URL.
- Tracking and attribution settings.
- Verification method for each reward objective.
- Fixed fees, performance rewards, tiered rewards, and bonuses.
- Reward and campaign budget limits.
- Content review requirements.
- Usage-rights and content-licensing terms where applicable.
- Submission requirements and evidence rules.
- Cancellation, correction, dispute, and late-event policies.
- Campaign owner and authorised reviewers.

Campaign controls:

- Save draft, preview, publish, pause, resume, close, and archive.
- Validate required fields before publication.
- Display unfunded or unconfigured reward obligations before a campaign is made available.
- Maintain an immutable or versioned record of accepted campaign terms.
- Record term changes, the person making each change, timestamps, and the effective scope.
- Prevent silent retroactive changes to terms already accepted by participants.

## 6.4 Campaign public page and creator page

CreatorPay separates three experiences:

### Public consumer landing page

Used when a business chooses to route creator traffic through a branded campaign page.

It can show the business logo, campaign banner, product description, a clear call to action, and the relevant destination. CreatorPay records supported visits or clicks and then sends the consumer to the business destination. The consumer does not need a CreatorPay account.

The public page is optional. A business may use a direct tracked redirect if that better suits the campaign.

### Authenticated creator campaign page

Used by creators who need to review the opportunity and its terms. It shows eligibility, campaign objective, deadlines, content requirements, compensation, attribution and verification rules, application or join controls, and the creator's campaign status.

After the creator is accepted or joins under the campaign's rules, the page provides the creator-specific tracking assets and submission workflows.

### Business campaign workspace

Used by authorised business and agency users to configure campaign terms, manage participants, review submissions, monitor events, inspect metrics, calculate rewards, and coordinate settlement.

These pages must not be collapsed into one page with conflicting permissions. Public consumers, creators, and business team members have different information needs and access rights.

## 6.5 Creator profiles and participation

- Creator account and profile.
- Contact details and professional information.
- Social handles and links.
- Content niches, platforms, languages, and relevant public portfolio links.
- Optional profile verification indicators where a supported method exists.
- Campaign invitations and invitation responses.
- Browse campaigns that are made discoverable.
- Apply to eligible campaigns.
- Accept campaign terms and record the version accepted.
- View campaign briefing and deliverables.
- Decline or withdraw where the campaign rules permit.
- See application/participation status.
- Track current obligations and deadlines.
- Access their own tracking links or codes.
- View personal metrics and reward history.
- Export or share selected campaign history where the record supports the claim.

Creator-provided social statistics must be labelled as self-reported unless corroborated through a supported source. Do not state that an account, audience, or metric is verified without a real verification process.

## 6.6 Invitations and applications

- Invite a creator by email or shareable invitation.
- Batch invitations to a selected creator list.
- Track invitation sent, delivered where available, viewed where available, accepted, declined, or expired.
- Publish a campaign opportunity for eligible creators to apply.
- Collect applications and relevant supporting information.
- Review and accept or reject applications.
- Record the selection decision and relevant reason.
- Prevent participation when eligibility rules fail.
- Require acceptance of applicable terms before participation becomes active.
- Avoid generating an active tracking link before the creator is eligible to participate, unless the campaign explicitly supports a test or prelaunch link state.

## 6.7 Briefs, deliverables, and content review

- Define each deliverable type, quantity, deadline, platform, format, and required evidence.
- Attach creative briefs, brand guidelines, required messages, prohibited claims, and references.
- Collect public post URLs, uploaded content, screenshots, files, and other allowed evidence.
- Record submission timestamps and revision history.
- Track draft, submitted, under review, revision requested, approved, rejected, and withdrawn states.
- Support business feedback and revision requests.
- Record approval/rejection decisions and explanations.
- Display deadlines and overdue items.
- Verify public post links where a supported check is available.
- Record required content usage-rights terms separately from performance or payment status.
- Retain a history of revisions rather than silently overwriting important evidence.

A content submission being approved does not automatically mean that a sales conversion is verified. Content delivery and performance outcomes are separate objects with separate rules.

## 6.8 Tracking links and click analytics

- Generate a creator-specific link for a campaign participation record.
- Use opaque or safely generated tracking tokens rather than exposing internal IDs or sensitive data.
- Redirect the visitor to the campaign's approved destination.
- Record eligible link clicks and timestamps.
- Report raw and eligible clicks separately where needed.
- Estimate unique visitors using a privacy-conscious method, explaining that unique-click calculations are estimates and can be affected by devices, browsers, and consent settings.
- Store relevant attribution information within defined retention periods.
- Support permitted UTM parameters or other destination parameters.
- Allow authorised link pause or disable actions.
- Validate destination URL changes and record them.
- Detect obvious automated traffic and suspicious click patterns.
- Keep invalidated clicks out of reward calculations according to documented criteria.
- Support QR/link presentation if demanded by customers.
- Provide creator and campaign-level click reporting.

Tracking clicks alone does not prove that a customer registered, enquired, or purchased.

## 6.9 Conversion events and integrations

Eligible events can include registrations, leads, completed applications, purchases, orders, subscriptions, or other campaign-specific actions. The business must define the event meaning before the campaign launches.

Supported event sources can include:

1. Authenticated server-to-server API integration.
2. Supported webhook or partner integration.
3. Promotional code or coupon records supplied by the business.
4. CSV or structured data imports that can be reconciled.
5. Manual evidence/review where automatic event tracking is unavailable.

Each event should have, where applicable:

- A unique external event ID or idempotency key.
- A defined event type.
- Campaign and attribution references.
- Event timestamp and received timestamp.
- Source/integration identity.
- Relevant non-sensitive value or currency fields where the campaign requires them.
- Verification status and evidence reference.
- Eligibility decision and explanation.
- Reversal, refund, or cancellation references where applicable.

The system should provide duplicate-event detection, schema validation, authentication checks, attribution-window checks, permission checks, and failed-event diagnostics. The event model should preserve source provenance so that a manually imported claim is not presented as equivalent to a securely verified server-side event.

## 6.10 Attribution rules

Attribution must be configured and disclosed before campaign participation begins.

The platform must support a defined default and document any campaign-specific alternatives that are allowed. A proposed initial default is **last eligible tracked creator click within the configured attribution window**, unless the customer selects an explicitly supported alternative. This is a product policy to validate, not a claim that last-click attribution represents causality perfectly.

Rules must cover:

- Attribution window.
- How last eligible click or another supported method is selected.
- Repeat clicks by the same visitor.
- Clicks involving multiple creator links.
- Duplicate event submissions.
- Events received late.
- Campaign closing while events are still pending.
- Refunds, cancellations, and chargebacks for purchases.
- Self-referrals or suspiciously related traffic when the available evidence supports review.
- Invalid click filtering.
- Manual event corrections and audit history.
- What happens when attribution information is missing or contradictory.

When attribution is unknown, the event must remain unattributed or require review. The platform must not assign it to a creator merely to complete a report.

## 6.11 Event verification and eligibility

An event can pass through separate states:

- **Received:** The system has accepted the event payload.
- **Reported:** The event is recorded but has not completed verification.
- **Under review:** Automated checks or a reviewer are evaluating the event.
- **Verified:** The event's evidence/source has passed the applicable verification checks.
- **Eligible:** The verified event meets this campaign's reward and attribution rules.
- **Rejected:** The event failed an identified check or was found invalid; reason is recorded.
- **Reversed/adjusted:** A previously accepted event requires correction, such as a refund or documented error.

Verification and eligibility are separate. A real purchase can be verified but not eligible if it occurred outside the reward window or violates a disclosed campaign condition. Conversely, an event marked eligible should have supporting evidence and a recorded rule evaluation.

## 6.12 Reward models and calculation

CreatorPay must support different compensation structures.

### Fixed deliverable fee

The creator earns a specified amount after completing the agreed deliverable and satisfying the campaign's approval conditions.

### Performance reward

The creator earns a specified amount for each eligible event or outcome, subject to campaign limits and anti-duplication rules.

### Hybrid reward

The creator receives a fixed deliverable fee plus performance rewards.

### Tiered and milestone reward

A creator earns a predefined bonus after reaching a specified eligible threshold, provided the campaign terms explain whether thresholds are incremental or retroactive.

### Multi-objective campaign

A campaign may reward more than one type of result—for example, content approval plus verified registrations. The reward model must state each component and whether the same event can contribute to more than one component. By default, duplicate counting should be prohibited unless the rules explicitly permit it.

Reward engine capabilities:

- Reward-component configuration.
- Eligibility conditions and caps.
- Fixed fees and unit-based rewards.
- Tiered rewards and milestone bonuses.
- Currency and rounding rules.
- Calculation preview.
- Reward budget monitoring.
- Creator-facing breakdown of input events and calculations.
- Business review and approval controls.
- Calculation version and input history.
- Documented adjustments/recalculations.
- Detection of duplicate reward claims.
- Separate record for fixed delivery fees and performance bonuses.

The platform must not force campaigns into performance-only compensation. A creator can fulfil valid work even if the product, checkout, or attribution setup prevents measurable sales. Campaign terms determine compensation, and fixed fees should not be erased merely because performance objectives were not reached.

## 6.13 Reward, funding, and settlement records

The platform must distinguish reward entitlement from money movement. An amount being calculated is not the same as a payout being initiated or completed.

Suggested reward statuses:

1. **Calculated** — formula applied to current eligible evidence.
2. **Pending review** — business or verification approval is needed.
3. **Approved** — authorised business approval recorded.
4. **Payable** — applicable funding and settlement conditions are met.
5. **Payment initiated** — request sent to the payment provider.
6. **Paid** — provider has confirmed a successful payment.
7. **Payment failed** — provider reports failure; retry or correction may be required.
8. **Disputed** — a specific amount or underlying event is under dispute.
9. **Adjusted/reversed** — an authorised correction was made with reason and audit history.

Campaign budget/funding states must be separate from individual reward states. A campaign can be published, paused, or closed while rewards remain pending settlement. The UI must not imply that an approved reward has already been paid.

Required capabilities:

- Campaign reward obligation summary.
- Approved, pending, disputed, and paid amount views.
- Business approval and permissions.
- Provider payout initiation and callback handling.
- Idempotent payout requests to avoid double payment.
- Provider references, failure reasons, and reconciliation.
- Creator payout history and relevant receipts/references.
- Dispute workflow with supporting evidence and decisions.
- Complete audit history for manual adjustments.

**Payment provider and custody model remain an open business/legal decision.** CreatorPay should not market escrow, safeguarded funds, or guaranteed payout until a provider arrangement and applicable legal position have been selected and verified.

## 6.14 Business analytics and reporting

Reports should include relevant available metrics, such as:

- Campaign budget and reward obligations.
- Creator participation and completion rates.
- Deliverables due, submitted, approved, rejected, or awaiting revision.
- Raw and eligible clicks.
- Verified and eligible conversions by event type.
- Attributable registrations, leads, and purchases where evidence exists.
- Performance by creator, link, campaign, content type, and time period where data supports it.
- Cost per eligible outcome.
- Reward calculations, disputes, and settlement statuses.
- Campaign comparisons using consistent definitions.
- CSV exports or shareable reports with appropriate permissions.

Return on ad spend or ROI must be displayed only when the necessary revenue/cost data is available. If a campaign can only prove clicks or registrations, the system must not fabricate purchase revenue or ROI.

## 6.15 Creator analytics and history

- Own clicks and eligible traffic.
- Own attributable event counts by type and status.
- Content submission and approval history.
- Reward component breakdown.
- Pending, approved, disputed, paid, and adjusted amounts.
- Campaign history.
- Relevant reviewer comments.
- Exportable selected records when supported.

Creators must not have access to other creators' private analytics or the business's confidential campaign-wide data unless that information is explicitly shared.

## 6.16 API, webhooks, and integrations

The API is a product capability, not a separate mandatory fee stream in the business model. Access and usage limits can be included in the appropriate business or agency subscription plans.

Capabilities:

- Create/manage campaigns where the account is authorised.
- Create/retrieve tracking links.
- Ingest eligible events.
- Retrieve event verification and eligibility statuses.
- Retrieve campaign performance summaries.
- Retrieve reward summaries and statuses where permitted.
- Receive webhooks for relevant event, review, reward, and payment state changes.
- Manage API credentials and permitted scopes.
- View integration delivery logs and failed requests.
- Support idempotent event submissions.
- Rate limiting and authentication.

The dashboard and API must use the same campaign, attribution, verification, and reward rules. An event must not earn a different reward simply because it arrived through a different interface.

## 6.17 Notifications

- Campaign invitation and response.
- Application status.
- Terms accepted and participation activated.
- Upcoming and missed deadlines.
- Submission received, revision requested, approved, or rejected.
- Event review or verification status updates where relevant.
- Reward calculation and approval updates.
- Payment initiated, failed, or completed.
- Campaign paused or closed.
- API/integration errors for authorised business users.

Notifications should be actionable, appropriately timed, and configurable. Avoid sending sensitive reward or account information into channels that are not appropriate for it.

## 6.18 Trust, security, and administration

- Role-based access control.
- Business/agency tenant isolation.
- Secure authentication and session management.
- Audit records of campaign-term changes, review decisions, reward adjustments, and payouts.
- Event-source provenance.
- Duplicate-event and suspicious-activity checks.
- Rate limiting and abuse monitoring.
- Data access/deletion processes consistent with legal obligations.
- Secure file upload and content access.
- Monitoring for failed event processing and payout callbacks.
- Support case and dispute handling.
- Administrative actions logged and limited to authorised staff.

Fraud detection should produce evidence and review signals, not label people as fraudulent without a documented basis. The platform must provide protections against bad actors on both sides: invalid claims from creators and arbitrary or inconsistent decisions by businesses.

# 7. Core user journeys

## 7.1 Business: first campaign

1. The business creates an account and workspace.
2. It completes basic organisation information and sets up the team roles it needs.
3. It creates a campaign and defines the objective, deliverables, eligible creators, destination, schedule, verification method, reward rules, and attribution window.
4. It previews campaign terms and checks for missing event or payment configuration.
5. It publishes or invites creators according to the chosen participation model.
6. It invites existing creators or reviews campaign applications.
7. Selected creators accept the applicable terms; the platform stores the accepted terms version.
8. CreatorPay creates the creator-campaign participation records and the relevant tracking assets.
9. Creators publish content and submit required links or evidence.
10. Clicks and supported events are received. The business monitors the campaign.
11. The business reviews deliverables and any events that require manual review.
12. CreatorPay calculates eligible rewards under the agreed rules.
13. Authorised users review and approve applicable amounts.
14. Settlement is coordinated through the selected provider; payment statuses are reconciled.
15. The business reviews final performance and can reuse the workflow for its next campaign.

## 7.2 Creator: invited to a campaign

1. The creator opens an invitation and signs in or creates an account.
2. The creator reads the objective, eligibility, deliverables, deadline, reward structure, tracking rules, verification approach, and relevant content terms.
3. The creator accepts or declines the invitation.
4. If accepted, the creator obtains the creator-specific tracking link or code.
5. The creator publishes and submits the agreed content.
6. The creator views submission and review status.
7. The creator sees clicks and attributed events using the status labels that match the evidence.
8. The creator sees how fixed fees and performance rewards are calculated.
9. The creator can review disputed or rejected amounts and follow the documented process.
10. The creator sees settlement updates and payment history.

## 7.3 Creator: applying to a public opportunity

1. The creator browses campaign opportunities made discoverable by businesses.
2. The creator checks eligibility, terms, requirements, and deadlines.
3. The creator submits an application and any requested links or profile details.
4. The business reviews and accepts or rejects the application.
5. If accepted, the creator must accept the relevant terms before participation becomes active.
6. Tracking and submission workflows then follow the same rules as an invited creator.

## 7.4 Consumer: follows a creator link

1. The consumer clicks a creator-specific campaign link.
2. CreatorPay validates the link and records the supported click event under the applicable privacy and retention rules.
3. The consumer is sent to the business's destination directly, or via an optional branded public campaign page.
4. The consumer does not need a CreatorPay account.
5. If the business later sends a supported conversion event or provides another accepted source of evidence, the platform attempts attribution under the campaign's configured rules.

The consumer journey must not be represented as a complete conversion path when CreatorPay only records a click.

## 7.5 Agency: manages multiple client campaigns

1. The agency creates an agency workspace.
2. It is connected to or invited into a client's workspace with explicit permissions.
3. It creates or manages campaigns for the authorised client.
4. It invites creators, manages submissions, reviews results, and prepares client-facing reports.
5. Data, budgets, reward obligations, and reports remain associated with the correct client.
6. The agency can compare its own authorised campaign operations without accessing unrelated clients' private data.

## 7.6 Business: integrates conversion events

1. An authorised user creates API credentials with limited scopes.
2. The business follows the event schema and tests with non-production or clearly marked test events.
3. The platform validates authentication, required fields, campaign eligibility, event timestamps, and idempotency keys.
4. Test events are labelled as tests and excluded from live reward calculations.
5. The business confirms successful ingestion and any expected attribution behaviour.
6. Live events are sent from the business's system.
7. Failed events are visible with actionable error information and can be retried safely.
8. The business can retrieve event status and campaign analytics through the API or dashboard.

# 8. Product and campaign rules

## 8.1 Campaign states

Suggested states:

- **Draft:** editable and not visible to participants.
- **Ready to publish:** required fields validated; funding/payment requirements checked according to the selected policy.
- **Published:** available to the permitted audience.
- **Paused:** new participation or specified activity temporarily stopped, according to campaign settings.
- **Closed:** no new eligible participation or events except late events explicitly allowed by the campaign rules.
- **Archived:** retained for history; normal campaign operations have ended.

Campaign closure does not erase outstanding submissions, eligible events, disputes, reward obligations, or settlement work.

## 8.2 Participation states

- Invited.
- Invitation accepted.
- Applied.
- Under review.
- Accepted/active.
- Declined.
- Rejected.
- Withdrawn.
- Suspended or removed under recorded policy.
- Completed.

The exact state transitions must be constrained by permissions and campaign configuration. A creator must not be considered an active participant before required terms are accepted.

## 8.3 Submission states

- Draft (optional if draft submission is supported).
- Submitted.
- Under review.
- Revision requested.
- Approved.
- Rejected.
- Withdrawn.

An approved submission should preserve who approved it, when, and against which requirements/terms. An approved submission is not automatically a verified conversion event.

## 8.4 Event states

Use the event lifecycle described in Section 6.11. Received, verified, and eligible are distinct states. The business may configure manual review for selected event types, but should not change event eligibility rules retroactively for already accepted campaign terms.

## 8.5 Reward states

Reward calculation, approval, payable status, and payment status must be distinct. A successful API request to initiate payment does not count as paid. The product should record the provider's confirmed result and reconcile failures.

## 8.6 Term changes

- Every accepted campaign term version must be preserved.
- The platform records who changed a term and when.
- Changes should clearly define whether they apply only to future participants, future events, or all participants after they explicitly accept the revised terms.
- Earned or approved amounts must not be silently decreased by a normal campaign-edit action.
- Corrections to erroneous calculations require a reason, a traceable adjustment, and the relevant dispute/appeal process.

## 8.7 Attribution and duplicate handling

- Every conversion event should be deduplicated using a reliable external ID or idempotency key where available.
- When duplicate detection is uncertain, the event may require review rather than automatic reward.
- Attribution follows the campaign's disclosed model and window.
- Unattributed events stay unattributed unless sufficient evidence is provided and a permitted review process resolves them.
- Late events are handled according to explicit campaign rules, not ad hoc preferences.
- Refunds/cancellations should adjust eligible purchase rewards where the original terms allow it.

## 8.8 Campaign funding policy — decision needed

The product must specify when funding is required. Two possible policies are:

- **Fund before publication:** appropriate for campaigns where CreatorPay or the selected provider must confirm funds before the business invites creators.
- **Publish before funding, but block payable rewards until funded:** lowers onboarding friction but increases the risk that an approved reward cannot be settled promptly.

This is not finalised. Choose one policy after provider, commercial, and compliance review. The interface must state campaign funding status clearly, and the reward calculation must not imply that money is available when it is not.

## 8.9 Public page versus direct redirect — supported choice

The business can choose between a branded CreatorPay public campaign page and a direct tracked redirect, subject to product policy and measurement requirements. Neither route should require the consumer to create a CreatorPay account. The system must preserve attribution identifiers across the chosen path and explain when tracking cannot continue to a downstream conversion.

## 9.5 Event-processing flow

1. Receive event from an authenticated and authorised source.
2. Validate schema and event type.
3. Check idempotency key or external event ID.
4. Validate campaign state, event timestamp, and relevant attribution metadata.
5. Store source provenance and initial status.
6. Run automated eligibility and fraud checks that can be applied safely.
7. Route uncertain or manual-review events to the review queue.
8. Update attribution/verification status.
9. Recalculate the affected reward components idempotently where policy allows.
10. Record audit history and notify relevant users when appropriate.

No event should create more than one reward for the same rule unless campaign terms explicitly allow it. Reprocessing a webhook or retrying an event should not duplicate events, rewards, or payouts.

# 10. Non-functional requirements

## 10.1 Correctness and financial integrity

- Monetary values use decimal-safe representations, not binary floating-point arithmetic.
- Currency, rounding rules, and reward formula versions are explicit.
- Every approved reward is traceable to source evidence and the rules applied.
- Payout requests are idempotent.
- Payment status is based on provider confirmation and reconciled records.
- Corrections create adjustment records rather than erasing history.

## 10.2 Security and permissions

- Enforce workspace and campaign-level permissions on the backend, not only in the frontend.
- Protect integration credentials and webhook secrets.
- Validate uploaded content and use access-controlled storage.
- Keep consumer tracking identifiers opaque and avoid unnecessary personal data.
- Log privileged operations.
- Separate test and production events.

## 10.3 Reliability and observability

- Track event ingestion failures and processing backlog.
- Retry transient failures with backoff and idempotency.
- Monitor payment-provider callback failures and payout reconciliation differences.
- Maintain automated database backups and tested restore processes.
- Provide clear integration diagnostics without leaking secrets.

## 10.4 Performance and scalability

- Redirect handling should be fast and resilient; heavy analytics work should be asynchronous.
- Use pagination for large event, submission, and audit lists.
- Index common queries by workspace, campaign, creator participant, status, and timestamp.
- Cache only data where stale values will not cause financial or permission errors.
- Scale workers independently if event load justifies it.

## 10.5 Privacy and retention

- Collect only data needed to operate the campaign and support measurement.
- Document what click, event, and submission data are stored and for how long.
- Limit access to campaign and creator information.
- Provide processes for applicable data-access, correction, and deletion requests, subject to legal and financial record obligations.
- Do not expose consumer identity to creators unless it is essential, legally appropriate, and specifically supported by the product policy.

## 10.6 Accessibility and usability

- Clear statuses and next actions.
- Usable responsive interface on desktop and mobile browsers.
- Accessible colour contrast and form validation.
- Plain-language explanations for attribution and reward calculations.
- Distinguish estimates, self-reported statistics, verified events, and paid rewards visually and textually.

# 11. Business model

## 11.1 Primary revenue streams

CreatorPay has two planned revenue streams.

### A. Business subscriptions

Recurring monthly or annual subscriptions for businesses using CreatorPay to manage campaigns, coordinate deliverables, track supported events, review results, and administer reward workflows.

Subscription tiers may eventually differ by active campaigns, seats, event volume, reporting, and workspace capability. API access is a product capability within the relevant plan, not automatically a separate revenue stream.

### B. Agency plans

Recurring plans for agencies managing campaigns across multiple client businesses. Agency plans may include client workspace separation, team permissions, reporting, and higher operational capacity.

## 11.2 Who does not pay?

Creators do not need a paid subscription to participate in campaigns and access the results or reward records relevant to them. This lowers participation friction and aligns the main subscription with the business-side value.

## 11.3 Pricing research, not final prices

Indicative local plans discussed internally have ranged from roughly ₦50,000–₦100,000 per month for smaller accounts and ₦150,000–₦300,000 per month for more demanding business accounts, with custom pricing for agencies. These are hypotheses, not established prices. Do not publish them until interviews and paid pilots validate willingness to pay, gross margin, and fit with account size.

For market modelling, the deck uses separate annual revenue assumptions for Nigeria and wider international markets. Those assumptions must be tested; they are not evidence of achieved average revenue per account.

## 11.4 Campaign funds are not CreatorPay revenue

Campaign budgets and creator compensation that CreatorPay helps administer must be accounted for separately from subscription revenue. If the product eventually charges any transaction-related fee, it must be transparent and supported by the selected provider/compliance model. No percentage-of-creator-earnings revenue stream is assumed in this strategy.

## 11.5 Customer lifetime value

Do not quote LTV yet. Calculate it when there is sufficient data on customer acquisition cost, gross margin, retention, churn, and expansion revenue.

# 13. Competitive landscape and differentiation

## 13.1 Direct and adjacent competitors

### AdMinting

AdMinting publicly promotes performance-oriented influencer marketing in Nigeria, creator onboarding/discovery, click/lead/conversion tracking, creator scoring, and a workflow for bringing existing creators. Source: [AdMinting — Performance Influencer Marketing](https://adminting.com/influencer-marketing).

**Implication:** CreatorPay cannot claim uniqueness merely by tracking clicks, leads, or sales, or by allowing a brand to bring existing creators.

### Posse

Posse publicly describes a platform combining African-market creator discovery, campaign management, content approvals, platform data, analytics, creator profiles, and AI-assisted campaign insights. Source: [Posse platform](https://www.posse.africa/platform/) and [Posse homepage](https://www.posse.africa/).

**Implication:** Campaign workflow, analytics, creator profiles, and African-market focus already overlap with the category.

### Beza

Beza publicly promotes creator discovery, campaign management, content review, tracking, pricing features, and payment/escrow positioning. Source: [Beza](https://bezahq.com/).

**Implication:** Creator discovery and payment clarity are not enough by themselves to differentiate CreatorPay.

### Modash

Modash publicly promotes creator discovery and management, automated content/performance tracking, tracking links and discount codes, Shopify sales attribution, reporting, and creator payment workflows. Source: [Modash influencer tracking](https://www.modash.io/features/influencer-tracking) and [Modash help centre](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13714522-what-is-modash-and-how-does-it-work).

**Implication:** Mature global platforms already cover much of the basic workflow. CreatorPay must identify customers for whom its positioning, local market fit, workflow, and pricing are meaningfully better.

### Manual alternatives and agencies

Businesses may use spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email, UTMs, promo codes, website analytics, or outsourced agencies. These can be inexpensive and familiar, even if reporting requires significant manual effort.

**Implication:** The competitor is not only other software. The product must demonstrate enough value to overcome inertia and the perceived cost of switching.

## 13.2 Intended competitive wedge

CreatorPay should centre its product around the commercial record connecting:

**Accepted campaign terms → delivered work → attributable evidence → reward calculation → settlement record.**

The intended emphasis is:

1. **Rule clarity:** Terms, eligible events, caps, and reward formulas are defined before participation.
2. **Evidence provenance:** Results show where the evidence came from and how it was verified.
3. **Transparent calculation:** Fixed work fees and performance rewards are separated and explainable.
4. **Auditability:** Term changes, review decisions, reward adjustments, and payment outcomes leave a traceable history.
5. **Practical integration:** Businesses can bring existing creators and send supported events from the systems they already use.

These points define our desired position, not a proven competitive moat. Public pages do not reveal every competitor capability. The team must test competitors directly and conduct customer interviews before claiming that CreatorPay does something no other product can do.

## 13.3 What we can implement from competitors

CreatorPay should not reject familiar features just because competitors offer them. Creator discovery, applications, content review, analytics, discount-code support, automated social data, and creator profile management may be valuable where they serve our target customer. The decision should depend on customer demand and differentiation strategy, not a desire to look different at all costs.

The core invariant is that these capabilities should feed the accountability and reward workflow rather than distract from it.

# 14. Go-to-market strategy

## 14.1 Acquisition principle

**Win businesses already spending on creators. Let those businesses bring the creators. Use successful campaigns to create repeat usage, referrals, and agency distribution.**

We should not make broad creator marketplace liquidity the first condition for product usefulness. The first businesses can invite creators they already trust.

## 14.2 Stage 1 — First 20 business conversations/design partners

Purpose: understand the problem before overbuilding.

Activities:

- Interview at least 20 businesses that have recently run creator campaigns, with a focus on the person responsible for the budget and reporting.
- Ask to walk through recent real campaigns and review redacted briefs, reports, tracking links, and reconciliation processes where customers consent.
- Interview creators separately to identify term clarity, reporting, and settlement frustrations.
- Identify which outcomes are observable and which require unsupported claims.
- Recruit 3–5 design partners willing to run live campaigns through the proposed workflow.
- Measure current reporting/reconciliation time and how customers currently decide which creators to rehire.

Success criteria should be defined before each pilot. A useful result includes willingness to pay or a credible path to a paid account, not simply positive interview feedback.

## 14.3 Stage 2 — First 100 business accounts

Purpose: turn pilots into a repeatable sales and onboarding process.

Activities:

- Convert successful pilots to paid accounts.
- Produce case studies using measured, attributable outcomes.
- Conduct outbound sales to businesses resembling the strongest design partners.
- Build referrals from existing customers and participating creators.
- Standardise campaign templates, onboarding, and integration documentation.
- Track sales cycle, conversion rate, retention, support burden, and campaign repeat rate.

Define the 100-account milestone explicitly as **paying business/agency accounts** if that is the fundraising metric. Do not mix free sign-ups with paying customers.

## 14.4 Stage 3 — First 500 business accounts

Purpose: scale without requiring founder intervention for every campaign.

Activities:

- Develop agency partnerships that expose CreatorPay to multiple client accounts.
- Improve self-service onboarding and product education.
- Use customer referrals and documented case studies.
- Prioritise integrations requested repeatedly by paying businesses.
- Establish a lightweight customer-success function.
- Create scalable support for event setup, reward reconciliation, and disputes.
- Expand beyond Nigeria only when demand and operational capability justify it.

The 500-account figure is an operating goal, not a prediction. Customer acquisition targets must be tied to sales capacity, product readiness, and cash runway.

## 14.5 Sales narrative

Lead with the customer's current campaign process, not a feature list. Ask how the business defines success, where data lives, how it attributes outcomes, how it calculates compensation, and how it decides whom to use again. Position CreatorPay as a way to make those repeated processes easier to account for.

At the assumed Year 3 account count and blended price, the year-end recurring-revenue run rate would be about $540,000 ARR, but this is a model output rather than a current result. The team should replace all placeholders with a budget based on the actual delivery plan before using it for fundraising.

# 18. Product risks and mitigations

## 18.1 Customers may not pay for better measurement

A business can acknowledge its reporting is messy and still choose spreadsheets or an agency. Validate willingness to pay through actual pilot commitments and paid conversion, not positive interview feedback alone.

## 18.2 Attribution is incomplete by nature

A customer may see a creator's content, ask questions in WhatsApp, and purchase via another channel. Without a suitable integration or accepted evidence, CreatorPay cannot prove that link. Distinguish clicks from conversions, use accurate confidence/status labels, and leave unsupported events unattributed.

## 18.3 Competitors already cover overlapping features

AdMinting, Posse, Beza, and Modash publicly promote substantial portions of creator campaign operations and measurement. Test their products and interview customers about gaps. Do not claim a feature is unique merely because CreatorPay has it.

## 18.4 Performance-only pay can unfairly shift risk to creators

Creators may fulfil content obligations while sales fail because of pricing, product quality, stock, checkout, delivery, or attribution limitations. Support fixed deliverable fees and hybrid compensation, and keep those fees separate from performance rewards.

## 18.5 Fraud or manipulated results can damage trust

Use idempotency, source provenance, eligibility checks, basic suspicious-activity detection, and review workflows. Provide reasons and appeal paths for rejected events. Do not allow a brand to silently alter terms or delete adverse records.

## 18.6 Payments and custody create operational/legal obligations

Select a payment provider and review the applicable legal, tax, and compliance requirements before promising escrow or payout guarantees. Keep software revenue separate from funds being moved for customers. Reconcile provider statuses instead of assuming that API success means payment success.

## 18.7 Product breadth can dilute the initial value

The complete product vision is broad. Validate the core workflow and the paying customer first. The first implementation should still use the same domain rules and states laid out here, even if some capabilities are introduced later.

# 19. Decisions that remain open

These are real decisions, not details to be silently guessed.

1. **Campaign funding policy:** Must funds be confirmed before publication, or can a campaign publish before being funded while reward settlement remains blocked? Select after provider and legal review.
2. **Payment provider and custody model:** Choose provider, payout routes, fees, failure/reconciliation handling, and any applicable custody/escrow legal structure.
3. **Attribution default:** Validate the default last-eligible-click model, attribution window, treatment of multiple creators, and supported overrides.
4. **First target segment:** Use customer interviews to decide whether the first concentrated segment is e-commerce/consumer products, digital applications/services, online education, events, or a particular agency segment.
5. **Initial pricing:** Test willingness to pay and the right dividing line between business subscriptions and agency plans.
6. **Public opportunity policy:** Decide which campaigns may be discoverable/apply-based and which remain invite-only.
7. **Content/platform data integrations:** Decide which social platforms and data access methods are commercially and technically available; do not assume every platform exposes every metric.
8. **Evidence retention and privacy:** Set a clear policy for click data, conversion event data, content submissions, and financial records with legal review.
9. **Market sizing:** Validate the number of target accounts and pricing assumptions before presenting TAM/SAM/SOM as more than a working model.
10. **Team and funding amount:** Confirm people, responsibilities, hiring needs, operating budget, and runway before completing Slide 10 or Slide 12.

# 20. FAQs and product boundaries

## Does every consumer need a CreatorPay account?

No. A consumer can click a creator's campaign link and visit the business destination without joining CreatorPay.

## Can CreatorPay track purchases that happen in a business's private systems automatically?

Only if the business provides suitable data through an integration, supported event source, code-based record, or another accepted evidence method. A tracked click alone does not prove a purchase.

## Can businesses bring creators they already work with?

Yes. This is central to the adoption strategy. Campaigns should work without requiring the brand to discover all creators through CreatorPay.

## Can a campaign combine fixed fees and sales bonuses?

Yes. Fixed deliverable fees and performance rewards are separate components with their own eligibility and approval logic.

## Can a business change campaign rules after launch?

Changes must be versioned and disclosed. Accepted terms and earned rewards cannot be silently rewritten. Changes may require participant acceptance depending on scope.

## Can CreatorPay guarantee a creator is paid?

The product can calculate rewards, record approvals, coordinate payout requests, and track provider status. Any guarantee or escrow claim depends on the actual funding and provider structure and must not be made in advance.

## Are the listed competitors missing CreatorPay's approach?

That is not established. The competitors' public pages show overlapping capabilities. CreatorPay's proposed emphasis is a connected, auditable commercial record, but the team must test competitors and customers before treating it as a differentiated advantage.

## Is CreatorPay only for Nigerian creators?

Nigeria is the initial commercial focus. The architecture and business model can support expansion into additional markets if the first market and target customer are validated.
